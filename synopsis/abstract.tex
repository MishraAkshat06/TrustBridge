TrustBridge is an AI-assisted, blockchain-enforced crowdfunding platform developed as a BTech final-year research prototype. Conventional crowdfunding platforms suffer from two fundamental limitations: contributors cannot reliably evaluate campaign quality or creator credibility prior to funding, and there is no programmable mechanism to enforce accountability after funding. TrustBridge addresses both limitations through an integrated architecture that combines Machine Learning (ML) campaign-success prediction, anomaly-based risk detection, Agentic AI explanation and evidence review, off-chain creator identity verification (KYC), and Solidity smart-contract escrow with milestone-based staged fund release.

At the time of campaign launch, a trained ML model --- evaluated across Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting classifiers --- estimates the probability of success using only launch-time features, thereby avoiding data leakage. An Isolation Forest model provides a complementary anomaly signal to flag statistically unusual campaigns. Four Agentic AI agents (Campaign Analyzer, Risk Analyst, Evidence Reviewer, and Explainer) synthesize these signals into human-readable assessments for contributors.

Contributor funds are held in a Solidity smart contract deployed on the Ethereum Sepolia testnet. The contract enforces a 20 ETH hard cap, releases an initial 20\% development tranche upon goal attainment, and gates the remaining 80\% across three subsequent milestone tranches. Each milestone requires the creator to submit evidence, which is analyzed by the Evidence Reviewer agent and then approved or rejected by an authorized human verifier. All financial events --- contributions, refunds, tranche releases --- are recorded on-chain and auditable via Etherscan.

The system is evaluated through four experiments: ML model comparison, explanation quality assessment, smart-contract correctness testing, and full end-to-end workflow validation. TrustBridge contributes a unified, research-ready prototype demonstrating that programmable escrow, AI-driven transparency, and human-verified milestones can meaningfully improve contributor trust and creator accountability in crowdfunding.
